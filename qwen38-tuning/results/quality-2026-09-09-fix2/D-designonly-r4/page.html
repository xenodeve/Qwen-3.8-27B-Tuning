01 Origin — 1998 +

Two PhD students, one garage, and a question: why does the web feel so hard to search?

In 1998, Larry Page and Sergey Brin — friends from Stanford's computer science program — were building a search engine called BackRub, named for the way it followed the links between pages. Their insight was simple and radical: a page's importance could be read from the links pointing at it.

They named the company after the mathematical term googol — a one followed by one hundred zeros — a nod to the mission of organizing an ocean of information. What began on a dozen borrowed servers became the default answer to 8.5 billion questions a day.

02 Timeline — 1998 → 2024 +
1998BackRub

The engine

PageRank goes live. A search engine that reads the web's own links, not just its keywords, starts beating the incumbents.

1999Founded

Google, Inc.

The company is incorporated. The first offices are a rented barn in Menlo Park, California.

2004IPO

Going public

Google lists on NASDAQ, opening at $100 a share. "Don't be evil" enters the code of conduct — and the cultural record.

2006Android

The quiet bet

Android is acquired for $50 million. A mobile operating system that will one day run on half the planet.

2012Scale

One billion

Android passes one billion installs. Chrome becomes the world's most-used browser. The web is now, largely, a Google surface.

2015Alphabet

The reorganization

Alphabet is born as a holding company — Google for the core, "Other Bets" for the moonshots: self-driving cars, loons, fiber.

2022Gemini

The model

Gemini arrives. The search box begins to meet the model, and the answer starts to be written, not just linked.

2024AI Overviews

The blue links change

AI Overviews roll out across search. Twenty-eight years after BackRub, the ten blue links begin to look different.
